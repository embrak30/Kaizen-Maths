A practical comparison

Kaizen Maths or Mathletics: start with your lesson needs.

A teacher resource workspace and a pupil practice platform solve different parts of the mathematics lesson.

What Mathletics does well

Mathletics combines personalised pupil activities, games and mastery challenges with automatic marking and progress reports. Its UK site offers school trials and a school quote route, as well as a separate home-user offer.

Where Kaizen Maths differs

Kaizen Maths is built around the teacher: choose a topic and level, generate fresh maths, show the worked steps, annotate the board and print a worksheet or starter. Its curriculum map includes GCSE, CSEC, CAPE and Oman GED alongside other routes. Pupils do not need accounts; pupil tasks, where enabled, use aliases and task codes.

Make It Count is a proposed development programme bringing the resource together with coaching and follow-up; participation depends on funding and agreement.

Pricing and the job you need done

Kaizen Maths teacher access is £9.99/month or £89/year after a 30-day trial. School access starts at £299/year for 1–5 teachers; this is not the same account scope as an unlimited-school licence.

Mathletics school pricing is quote-based on its UK site. Request a quote for your pupil and school requirements rather than comparing it with a single-teacher subscription.

Choose Mathletics to evaluate a pupil-facing practice and engagement workflow. Try Kaizen Maths for teacher-led explanations and printable resources. Both should be checked against your actual topics and class routines.

Sources checked 27 October 2026: Dr Frost features, Dr Frost pricing, Mathletics UK. Offers and coverage can change; check each provider before buying.
