From a strip to a volume

Select a region and an axis, then begin in Base plane. The chosen strip is perpendicular to the rotation axis. Rotate it through a full turn: a strip reaching the axis produces a disc; a strip away from the axis produces a washer. Use Face-on to identify the outer and inner radii. A washer’s area is πR² − πr², rather than π(R − r)².

Revolve controls the actual mathematical rotation. Orbit camera changes only the viewing angle. In Revolve, the selected strip sweeps out a section. In Solid, the whole region sweeps out a solid. The rotation slider allows the process to be paused at any angle. The area and volume formulas refer to the complete 360° revolution; the angle readout identifies a partial sweep.

The x-axis examples use vertical strips and dx. The y-axis examples use horizontal strips and dy, with boundaries written as x in terms of y. Changing axes selects the corresponding region described in the panel; it does not keep a disc region fixed. The two-curve washer example is the same region in either orientation.

All intervals are 0 to 1. Radii are non-negative on this interval. The illustrative slice thickness is 0.025 units, clipped to the upper limit. The diagram is sampled for drawing; volumes are evaluated exactly. Keyboard: focus the scene; arrows orbit, +/− zoom, Home resets the camera.
