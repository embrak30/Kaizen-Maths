THE REGION

The curves meet at (0, 0) and (1, 1). Between them, √x is above x². This shaded region is the base of our solid.

The base lies between y = √x and y = x², for 0 ≤ x ≤ 1. Each cross-section is perpendicular to the x-axis.

Teaching with the construction

Begin with the base plane. Ask students to identify the upper and lower curves, then explain why their difference is a length. Build one cross-section and use Face-on to identify its defining dimension. Change shapes while keeping x fixed to compare areas. Reveal the complete solid, then connect each slice’s area and thickness to the volume integral.

The example is fixed to y = √x and y = x². All lengths are in arbitrary units. Circles lie in planes x = constant: in diameter mode their centres lie midway along the segment; in radius mode their centres lie on y = x². Their full cross-sections extend on both sides of z = 0. The base is a reference region used to determine section sizes. The displayed solid is sampled for drawing; the volume is evaluated exactly.
