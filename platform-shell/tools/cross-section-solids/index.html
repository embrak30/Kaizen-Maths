Teaching with the construction

Begin with the base plane. Ask students to identify the upper and lower curves, then explain why their difference is a length. Build one cross-section and use Face-on to identify its defining dimension. Change shapes while keeping x fixed to compare areas. Reveal the complete solid, then connect each slice’s area and thickness to the volume integral.

Select two functions from the lists, or choose a teaching preset. The interval is set automatically: 0 to 1 for square roots, −π to π for trigonometric functions, and −1 to 1 otherwise. Intersections split the base into pieces. At each crossing, check which function is above before forming upper minus lower. Interval endpoints close pieces that do not end at intersections. All lengths are in arbitrary units. Circles lie in planes x = constant: in diameter mode their centres lie midway along the segment; in radius mode their centres lie on the lower curve. Their full cross-sections extend on both sides of z = 0. The base is a reference region used to determine section sizes. The displayed solid is sampled for drawing; custom volumes are evaluated numerically. Preset answers include exact values.

Keyboard: focus the graph and use arrow keys to orbit, + / − to zoom, and Home to reset. Respecting reduced-motion preferences, animations start only when requested.
