Teaching with OOO

Show the four cards and give pupils time to notice their properties. Select a card to highlight a proposed choice. Selecting it again clears the highlight. No card is marked right or wrong.

Ask pupils to name a property and check that the other three do not share it. Then invite another choice, a different explanation, or a counterexample. Every set has at least one checked justification for each card.

Start simple uses small positive numbers and familiar shape properties. Dig deeper includes negative numbers, larger numbers and overlapping shape classifications. “Surprise me” chooses a complete number or shape set; it does not mix unrelated objects within a set.

Use Teacher ideas after discussion, or to prepare beforehand. The ideas follow the current card positions. Shuffle positions keeps the same set and changes its arrangement. New set works through the matching bank before repeating. There are 28 sets: 16 number sets and 12 shape sets.

Shape drawings have sharp vertices and retain their proportions. A rotation does not change the shape’s mathematical properties. Shape names are optional. A square is also a rectangle and a rhombus; the named non-square examples are drawn with unequal adjacent sides or without right angles. Here, a trapezium has exactly one pair of parallel sides.

For number discussions, 0 is even, a square and a cube, but neither prime nor composite. Negative integers can be cubes. The triangular-number ideas refer to positive triangular numbers. Powers of 2 include 1 = 2⁰.

Keep colour, position and selection separate from mathematical properties: all four cards use the same visual style. A larger displayed numeral does not represent a larger font or greater area.

Keyboard: Tab to a card and press Enter or Space to select. Arrow keys move between cards. When no field or dialog has focus, A–D selects the corresponding card.
